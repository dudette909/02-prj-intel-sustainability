Milestones in progress

Scroll through the moments that shaped Intel's approach to a more responsible future.

1969

Intel's 3301 Schottky bipolar 1021-bit read-only memory, which stands for ROM. This was the successor to Intel's first product, the 3101 Schottky TTL bipolar 64-bit static random-access memory (SRAM), both released in 1969.

Schottky TTL bipolar

1971

Intel's first microchip processor, the Intel 4004 in 1971. This was also the first commercially available microprocessor. This microprocessor represented a notable advance in technology, especially circuitry.

Intel 4004 Microprocessor

1975

Intel had begun working on this project in 1975 and produced a highly advanced 32-bit microprocessor, and only later released it in 1981.

Intel iAPX 432

1978

The Intel 286, a 16-bit microprocessor that was introduced on February 1, 1982. It was the first 8086-based CPU with separate, non-multiplexed address and data buses and also the first with memory management and wide protection abilities.

Originally Intel 80286

1982

The Intel 386, renamed i386, a third generation x86 microprocessor developed by Intel. It's also the first 32-bit processor in line and the first to implement the full IA-32 microarchitecture.

The i386

1989

Representing the fourth generation of binary compatible CPUs following the 8086 of 1978. This was also the first x86 chip to include more than one million transistors.

Intel's i486

1993

The Pentium (also referred to as the i586 or P5 Pentium) is a microprocessor introduced by Intel on March 22, 1993. It is the first CPU using the Pentium brand and was a fifth generation processor.

The Pentium Brand

Name of image

First launched in 2006, Intel Core is a line of multi-core CPUs for a variety of computing uses. These processors displaced the existing mid- to high-end Pentium processors at the time of their introduction. The Core also consisted of single-core and dual-core models.

Intel Core

2011

The second generation of the Intel Core's family. It was the successor to the Nehalem, a 1st-generation Core processor, and was the predecessor to the third generation Ivy Bridge.

Sandy Bridge
